The Physics Behind Photography

Photography is not just art. It is science, physics and creativity working together.

01. Light & Reflection

Light travels in straight lines. When light hits an object, some light reflects into the camera lens. This is how we capture an image.

02. Refraction & Lenses

Camera lenses bend light through refraction. They focus incoming light onto the camera sensor to produce a sharp image.

03. Image Formation

A convex lens forms a real, inverted image on the camera sensor. The sensor converts light into digital information.

Photography Learning Guide

Start from zero and understand how professional photography works.

Lesson 01: Understanding Aperture

Aperture controls the opening inside the camera lens.

  • Large aperture: More light enters.
  • Small aperture: Less light enters.
  • Wide aperture: Usually creates a blurred background.
  • Small aperture: Usually increases depth of field.

Lesson 02: Understanding Shutter Speed

Shutter speed controls how long light reaches the camera sensor.

  • Fast shutter: Freezes moving objects.
  • Slow shutter: Captures motion blur.
  • Example: Photographing flowing water.
  • Experiment with different shutter speeds.

Lesson 03: Understanding ISO

ISO controls the camera sensor's signal amplification.

  • Low ISO: Less image noise.
  • High ISO: Better exposure in darker scenes.
  • Very high ISO: Can introduce visible noise.

Lesson 04: The Exposure Triangle

Aperture, shutter speed and ISO work together to control exposure.

  • Aperture controls lens opening.
  • Shutter speed controls exposure time.
  • ISO controls signal amplification.
  • Balance all three for a good photograph.
